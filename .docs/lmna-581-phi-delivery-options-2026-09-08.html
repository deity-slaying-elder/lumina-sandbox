Delivering a report that contains patient data

Why the standard Salesforce email route into Microsoft 365 is closed to us, what that actually means in plain terms, and a simpler alternative that removes the problem rather than working around it. Every rule cited below links to the vendor's own documentation.

Bottom line up front

The build is finished and switched off. The only open question is delivery, and the route almost everyone uses is blocked here by a Microsoft policy change rather than by anything in our setup.

  • Email Relay into Microsoft 365 will not pass. Microsoft now requires the sending system to prove its identity in a way Salesforce cannot.
  • Lumina's licensing is close, with one gap. Microsoft 365 F3 does not include message encryption on its own, and the licence has to sit on the account doing the sending.
  • Recommended: stop emailing the data. Email a link that expires instead. Salesforce has this built in, with a record of every view and download.

Background

Facilities need to know each morning which of their discharged patients went back to hospital. The report names patients, so it counts as protected health information.

The ask

A decision on how we deliver it. The batch, scheduler, recipient logic and tests are already written and tested.

Status

Code complete in the dev sandbox since 24 August. Scheduled 8am Mon to Fri, held by a kill switch pending this decision.

01

What Email Relay is, and why it is the normal answer

Worth setting out plainly, because this is genuinely the standard approach and it is what most Salesforce organisations running Microsoft 365 use. The objection here is specific to our situation, not to the feature.

Left alone, Salesforce sends email from its own servers. Fine for a password reset, but it means the message never passes through the client's own mail system, so none of the client's rules apply to it. No company signature, no archiving, and critically for us, no encryption.

Email Relay changes the exit door. Rather than posting the letter itself, Salesforce hands it to the client's mail server and lets that server post it. Everything the client's mail system normally does then happens, including applying their encryption rules. That is why it was the obvious first choice. Salesforce cannot encrypt to a HIPAA standard, but Lumina's Microsoft tenant already can, and already has.

It is configured on both sides, and most of the work is theirs

This is the part clients are sometimes surprised by. Roughly a quarter of the configuration sits in Salesforce. The rest is inside the client's own Microsoft tenant, and only their IT team can do it.

WhoWhat they configure
Us, in SalesforceAn Email Relay record holding the client's mail host, port and TLS setting. An email domain filter, without which the relay never activates. A verified sending address. Switching off Salesforce compliance headers and bounce management.
Client IT, in MicrosoftAn inbound connector that accepts mail from Salesforce. An allowlist of Salesforce's sending IP addresses. A rule restricting which Salesforce organisation may use it. DNS changes for SPF and DKIM. Then ongoing upkeep whenever Salesforce changes its addresses.

One more thing worth knowing before the next section. Salesforce has four ways to send mail, and only Email Relay covers the kind of email we are sending. The Microsoft 365 and Gmail integrations handle only mail a person types and sends by hand, which Salesforce documents explicitly. Our report comes from an automated job, so relay is the only option that can route it through the client's mail system at all.

  • Salesforce Help · the four options comparedBest Email Setup for Your Salesforce Organization
    Confirms Send through Office 365 and Send through Gmail cover only composer and certain API email, not workflow or system email, while Email Relay covers Salesforce generated mail.
  • Salesforce Help · relay configurationSet Up Email Relay
    The host, port and TLS fields, the supported ports, and the requirement for an email domain filter before a relay will activate.
02

Why it will not pass, explained properly

A mail server will not post letters for a stranger. If it did, spammers would use every company's mail server to blast the world. So before Microsoft accepts mail from an outside system and forwards it on, it insists on knowing who that system is.

The rule

Microsoft accepts two forms of ID

A TLS certificate carrying a domain name Microsoft recognises as the client's. Think of it as a company badge with Lumina's name on it.

Or a fixed IP address belonging to that client alone. Think of it as a permanent desk that only Lumina uses.

The problem

Salesforce carries neither

Salesforce is one shared building serving thousands of companies. Its mail leaves from IP ranges shared across all its customers, so the desk is not Lumina's.

And Salesforce does not issue a per customer certificate for email. Its TLS settings verify the certificate of the server it is connecting to, not one it presents itself. So there is no badge either.

What changed

A third form of ID was withdrawn

Until 1 November 2023 Microsoft also accepted the name printed on the letterhead, meaning the visible From address. That was enough to make this route work.

Anyone can print letterhead, so Microsoft stopped accepting it. Their documentation names our exact situation, a cloud service sending on a customer's behalf, as one broken by the change.

That is what the shorthand "their replacement needs a certificate Salesforce does not issue" means. Microsoft's written remedy for a third party service in this position is a unique certificate for the customer's organisation, whose domain the customer has registered with Microsoft. Salesforce does not offer that for email relay, so the remedy is not available to us. It is not a setting anyone has missed, and no Salesforce licence changes it.

03

Lumina's Microsoft licensing, and the one catch

Lumina confirmed a mix of Microsoft 365 Business Premium and Microsoft 365 F3, that Purview Message Encryption is enabled and has been tested to external recipients, and that Microsoft provides the HIPAA agreement through its Online Services terms. That settles the capability question. One detail does not hold up, and it matters because the failure is silent.

LicenceMessage encryption includedWhat it means here
Business PremiumYesWorks out of the box for these users.
Microsoft 365 F3No, add on requiredMicrosoft's documentation states Azure Information Protection Plan 1 can be added to F3 to receive Purview Message Encryption. On its own, F3 does not include it.
Why this is the risk to watch

Encryption is licensed on the sender, not the recipient, and Microsoft states each user benefiting from message encryption needs a licence. These reports send from a service address rather than a person, so that account needs its own entitlement. Lumina's successful test was almost certainly from a Business Premium account, which does not prove the service mailbox will encrypt.

If that mailbox lands on F3 without the add on, mail leaves unencrypted and nothing reports an error. No bounce, nothing to alert on. One test send from that specific address, triggered by a script rather than by a person in Outlook, settles it in minutes.

  • Microsoft Learn · licensingMessage Encryption FAQ
    Azure Information Protection Plan 1 can be added to Office 365 F3 to receive Purview Message Encryption, and each user benefiting from message encryption needs a licence.
  • Microsoft Learn · service descriptionMicrosoft Purview service description
    Per service licensing detail for Purview features across Microsoft 365 plans.

The recommended route, start to finish

If most recipients end up clicking a link either way, ours can be better than Microsoft's: one click, no sign in, and it expires on its own. The notification email carries no patient data at all, which is why none of the constraints above apply to it.

    Why this is the recommendation

    The email contains no patient data, so there is nothing to encrypt in transit and no reason to route it through anyone's mail system. That removes the connector, the certificate, the IP allowlist, the quarterly maintenance and the licensing gap in one move. It is not a cleverer way through the problem, it avoids having the problem.

    It also treats every recipient the same. Genesis on Google and the smaller facilities on Microsoft both get an identical single click, rather than 61% of recipients signing in to a Microsoft page every morning.

    What it costs, honestly

    Recipients click a link, so a custom domain is needed. A link on a generic Salesforce address, sent to healthcare staff trained not to click links in email, will be reported as suspicious. Something like reports.luminacare.com fixes that, and it is a DNS change plus a certificate.

    It also needs compliance sign off in writing. A link with no login authenticates by possession of the mailbox it was sent to, which is the same model Microsoft's own one time passcode flow uses, so it is accepted practice rather than novel. Adding one second factor, the facility postcode for example, makes that conversation straightforward.

    Optional extras, and what each one costs

    The seven steps above are the core. These are things we can switch on or build on top, and each needs a yes or no from Lumina rather than being assumed. The first one is the only one we would actively recommend.

    Recommended

    Export to CSV or PDF

    Needs Lumina's confirmation
    • Staff need to work the list, ring families and chase notes, not read it on a screen
    • Every download recorded separately from a view, so you can tell looking from taking
    • A logged export beats an unlogged screenshot, which is what people do otherwise
    • A copy of the data leaves Salesforce and lands on their device, where their own obligations govern it

    Second factor on open

    Optional, compliance may ask
    • One extra field, the facility postcode, before the report shows
    • Turns a forwarded link into a non event
    • Makes the compliance conversation shorter
    • One more step for the recipient every time

    Code required to download

    On request, stronger
    • A fresh code emailed at the moment of download
    • A leaked link could be viewed but not exported
    • Adds friction exactly where people are busiest
    • We would build it as a switch and leave it off unless asked

    Alert on every open

    Optional, native toggle
    • Salesforce can notify Lumina each time a report is opened
    • Useful during the first weeks to see whether facilities engage
    • Around 23 notifications a day, so likely worth turning off after launch
    On the export button specifically

    Nothing in HIPAA prevents a facility downloading data about its own former patients. They are a covered entity receiving information for treatment and operations, and once it reaches their device their own safeguards apply, exactly as they would if we had emailed a spreadsheet.

    The practical argument matters more. If someone can see the list on screen they can already copy it, by screenshot, by print, or by retyping it. Blocking export does not prevent a copy being made, it only prevents us knowing that one was. An export button that writes an audit record is the better position of the two.

    The one rule that is not negotiable is scope. An export must contain only that facility's own rows, enforced on the server rather than by what the page happens to display.

    • HHS · is this allowed at allDoes the Security Rule allow sending ePHI in an email?
      The Security Rule does not prohibit it, but requires access control, integrity and transmission security safeguards.
    • HHS · what counts as encryptedGuidance to render unsecured PHI unusable
      Names TLS per NIST SP 800-52 as a valid encryption process for data in motion, which is what HTTPS on the link page provides.
    • eCFR · the specific obligations45 CFR 164.312, Technical safeguards
      Audit controls and person or entity authentication are required specifications. Encryption of transmissions is addressable, meaning it must be implemented if reasonable and appropriate after a risk assessment, or an equivalent measure documented.
    • eCFR · the audit trail requirement45 CFR 164.528, Accounting of disclosures
      Individuals may request who received their information, what was disclosed and why. The per view records satisfy this. Email produces nothing comparable, you know only that it left the building.
    • Salesforce · contractual positionBAA Restrictions, last updated 31 July 2026
      Lists Lightning Platform including Force.com, Site.com and Experience Cloud among the HIPAA Covered Services, with no restriction anywhere on guest, unauthenticated or public access. Salesforce does write such restrictions when it means them, Tableau has an explicit one about public facing publication of PHI.
