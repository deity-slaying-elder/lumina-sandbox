Delivering the readmission report by expiring link

Every path the report can take, captured from the live sandbox after deployment. Each recipient gets their own link, so an open or a download is attributed to a named mailbox rather than only to a facility.

BUILT & LIVE-TESTED IN SANDBOX · NOT YET IN PRODUCTION
Scope, plainly. Deployed to lumDev on 9 September 2026, 74 of 74 tests passing, and exercised end to end with fabricated patient records. It is not in production, production holds none of this code, and the kill switch Is_Active__c is still false, so the 8am schedule is a no-op. Nothing has ever been sent to a real facility. Patient names in every screenshot are invented.

The happy path

Five steps, all captured from the sandbox run at 2:41pm ET on 9 September.

01

The job builds one report and one link per recipient

The nightly batch groups yesterday's readmissions by facility and renders that facility's report to PDF once. It then asks Salesforce for a separate public link per recipient, all pointing at the same file. Facilities with no readmissions are not emailed at all.

SettingValue in the tested run
Link lifetimeLink_Expiry_Hours__c = 24. Created 18:41 UTC, expires 2026-09-10 18:41 UTC. Measured at exactly 24.0 hours.
View capMax_Opens__c = 10. The link closes early once a recipient has opened it ten times.
Download allowedAllow_Download__c = true, logged separately from a view.
PasswordNone by design. The unguessable URL is the credential.
StorageOne PDF per facility, filed on the Facility record. Every recipient link points at that same file, so extra recipients cost one small row each.
02

Each recipient gets an email with no patient data in it

The message carries a count and a button, and names the address it was issued to. No patient names, no dates of birth, no attachment.

03

One click, no login, and the report opens

The recipient has no Salesforce account and never signs in. The long random string in the URL is the credential, and it unlocks that one file and nothing else. Captured in a browser profile with zero Salesforce cookies.

04

The open is attributed to that mailbox, not just the facility

This is the part facility-level tracking could not do. Because each link was issued to one address, the platform recording an open tells you who opened it. Below are the real rows after opening only the Gmail recipient's link.

05

Lumina gets one digest, not a copy of every email

Kevin receives a single summary naming each facility, each recipient, their role and the link expiry, followed by the day's open and download totals. Facilities that were skipped appear underneath, so gaps surface daily instead of silently.

Worth being exact, because this is the one place the design trades something away.

Identity here is possession of the mailbox the link was sent to, the same model Microsoft's own one-time-passcode encryption uses. It satisfies HIPAA accounting of disclosures, which asks for the entity and address that received the data.

The other paths

A single happy case is a demo, not proof. These are the branches that matter.

06

The link dies on its own, two ways

Whichever comes first: 24 hours, or ten opens. Anyone opening it after that, including anyone the email was forwarded to, gets Salesforce's page instead of the report.

Lumina can reissue a link, and that is the intended route

Salesforce's expired page carries no way back, so the email itself tells the recipient to reply and Lumina reissues it for them. Because every link now has its own row naming its recipient, a reissue is a lookup rather than a guess: staff open the Facility, see who held which link and whether they ever opened it, then send that person a fresh one. The new link always goes to the address on the row, never to whoever asked, so a forwarded request cannot redirect a report somewhere else.

Built and live in the sandbox. Resend Readmissions Report sits on the Facility record. It first shows what would go out, to which addresses and in which role, and sends nothing until that is confirmed. Confirming issues a brand new link per recipient, points it at the report already filed on the record rather than generating a second copy, and reports what actually sent.

07

A facility with no contact on file is skipped, loudly

If a facility has readmissions but no administrator or director-of-nursing email, nothing is sent and the reason is named in the digest rather than swallowed. Measured against production today: of the 131 facilities that had a qualifying readmission in the last 30 days, 25 have no contact of either kind, so about one report in five goes nowhere. Across the whole enrolled list the gap is wider: 159 of 326 facilities have neither address, 66 of them GHC and 60 Complete Care Management.

08

A misconfigured sender is reported, not fatal

Found by the live test rather than by reading the code. The first run reported Emails sent: 0 because the sending address existed but had never been verified, and Salesforce refuses to send from an unverified address.

BeforeAfter

A setup gap should never silence a daily clinical report. It should send anyway and complain in the summary, which is now what happens.

Proof of delivery

Both testers received both messages, each with their own link. This is a real inbox, not a mockup.

Where it lives in Salesforce

Every report is filed against the facility it belongs to, with the per-recipient links alongside it, so Lumina staff can see who received what and whether they read it.

Still needed before production

  1. Verify the sending address in productionCreate tcm@luminacare.com as an Org-Wide Email Address and have someone with that mailbox click the verification link. Production currently has none.
  2. Confirm the export button should be thereRecommended, since staff need to work the list rather than read it on screen, and every download is now logged against a named recipient. Worth an explicit yes because a copy leaves Salesforce.
  3. Confirm the view cap and the link lifetimeCurrently 24 hours and 10 opens per recipient. Both are configuration, changeable without a deploy.
  4. An owner for the facilities with no contact25 of the 131 facilities that had a readmission in the last 30 days have no administrator and no director-of-nursing email, and 159 of 326 enrolled facilities have neither. Concentrated in GHC and Complete Care Management. Independent of the delivery method, and the single biggest limit on coverage.
  5. Confirm the retention ruleThe stored PDF is now deleted once every link to that report has expired, after a grace period, so patient data does not outlive the delivery window on the record. A recipient's own download is theirs and is unaffected. Worth confirming the grace period, currently seven days.
  6. Compliance sign-off in writingThe link authenticates by possession of the mailbox. Worth recording rather than agreeing verbally.
  7. Decide whether IP addresses are wantedNot available on the free tier. It needs the Event Monitoring add-on licence, or a custom page we host.
